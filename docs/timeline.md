## 🗓️ Complete Economic Timeline

### 🔵 2016 — The First IMF Program

<details open>
<summary><strong>November 2016: Landmark IMF Deal & First Float</strong></summary>

| Date | Event | Impact |
|------|-------|--------|
| Nov 3, 2016 | EGP floated for first time | Rate: 8.8 → 17.5 EGP/USD overnight |
| Nov 11, 2016 | $12B IMF Extended Fund Facility signed | 3-year program, reform conditionality |
| Dec 2016 | Fuel subsidies cut by ~40% | Inflation spikes to 30%+ briefly |
| 2017–2018 | Stabilization period | Inflation falls; growth recovers to 5.3% |

**Outcome:** The 2016 program was considered a success — growth recovered to 5.3% by FY2018-19, inflation fell from 30%+ to ~10%, and reserves rebuilt to $44B by 2019. This set the template for the 2022–2024 episode.

</details>

### 🟡 2019–2021 — Pre-Crisis Stability

<details>
<summary><strong>The "Good Years" Before the Shocks</strong></summary>

| Period | Indicator | Value |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| FY2018-19 | GDP Growth | **5.6%** (highest in decade) |
| 2019 | Foreign Reserves | **$44.5B** (peak) |
| FY2019-20 | GDP Growth | **3.6%** (COVID impact) |
| 2020 | Tourism Collapse | From $13B → **$4B** (COVID) |
| FY2020-21 | GDP Growth | **3.3%** (recovered faster than peers) |
| 2021 | Suez Canal Blockage | Ever Given grounded for 6 days; $9.6B/day losses globally |
| 2021 | Inflation (annual) | **~5.3%** — benign era |

**Context:** Egypt entered the 2022 crisis from a position of relative strength but with known vulnerabilities:
- High dependence on tourism (which COVID decimated)
- Significant imports of wheat and energy
- External debt accumulation during reform period
- ~65% of government bonds held by foreign investors (hot money)

</details>

### 🔴 2022 — The Perfect Storm Hits

<details>
<summary><strong>February–December 2022: Russia-Ukraine Shock</strong></summary>

#### February 24, 2022: Russia Invades Ukraine
This single event triggered a cascade for Egypt:

1. **Wheat Crisis**: Egypt imports ~55-60% of wheat; Russia & Ukraine supply ~40% of global wheat. Prices spiked +60%.
2. **Capital Flight**: Global risk-off sentiment → foreign investors pulled $20B+ from Egyptian T-bills in weeks
3. **Energy Prices**: Oil spike increased Egypt's energy import bill
4. **Tourism**: Russian & Ukrainian tourists = ~30% of Egypt's visitors; both flows collapsed

| Month | Event | EGP/USD Rate |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| Jan 2022 | Pre-crisis | 15.7 |
| Mar 21, 2022 | **First devaluation** | 18.5 |
| May 2022 | IMF negotiations begin | 19.5 |
| Oct 27, 2022 | **Second devaluation** | 22.5 |
| Dec 2022 | IMF $3B EFF approved | 24.7 |

**CBE Response:**
- Raised rates by 300 bps in March 2022 (emergency)
- Further 200 bps in May 2022
- Import restrictions introduced (requiring letters of credit)
- These restrictions backfired — caused supply shortages, factory closures

</details>

### 🟠 2023 — Deepening Crisis

<details>
<summary><strong>January–December 2023: Inflation Peaks, Negotiations Stall</strong></summary>

| Month | Event | Key Data |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Jan 14, 2023 | **Third devaluation** | 15.7 → 30.9 EGP/USD |
| Jan 2023 | Inflation acceleration | Hits 25.8% (annual) |
| Feb 2023 | IMF 1st tranche delay | Program benchmarks missed |
| May 2023 | Food inflation peak | **71.4% YoY** |
| Sep 2023 | Headline inflation peak | **38%** — highest since 1986 |
| Oct 2023 | Gaza war begins | Regional risk premium rises; tourism bookings drop |
| Nov 2023 | Houthi attacks begin | Red Sea shipping threatened |
| Dec 2023 | Reserves at low | ~$33.5B |
| Dec 2023 | Ras El-Hekma negotiations | Talks with UAE ADQ begin |

**Parallel Market:**
- Official rate: ~30 EGP/USD
- Black market rate: 60–70 EGP/USD
- Gap signals fundamental misalignment — resolved only in March 2024

**Social Impact:**
- 5+ million households added to social protection programs
- Bread subsidy spending doubled in nominal terms
- Minimum wage raised from EGP 3,000 → EGP 6,000/month (still lagged inflation)

</details>

### 🟢 2024 — The Turning Point

<details open>
<summary><strong>January–December 2024: Stabilization & Reform Breakthrough</strong></summary>

| Date | Event | Significance |
|------|-------|-------------|
| **Feb 2024** | Ras El-Hekma deal signed | $35B UAE FDI; $15B upfront |
| **Mar 6, 2024** | **Fourth & largest devaluation** | 30.9 → 49.5 EGP/USD in single day |
| **Mar 2024** | CBE raises rates +600 bps | Benchmark rate hits 27% |
| **Mar 2024** | IMF $820M tranche released | Program back on track |
| **Mar 2024** | Parallel market collapses | Black market premium eliminated |
| **Apr 2024** | World Bank $500M | Social protection support |
| **Q2 2024** | Tourism rebound | Red Sea bookings recovering |
| **Jun 2024** | Privatization list expanded | 35+ SOEs listed for sale |
| **Sep 2024** | Inflation falling | From 38% peak → ~26% |
| **Dec 2024** | Reserves rebuilt | $35.2B (from $33.5B trough) |
| **Dec 2024** | IMF 2nd tranche | $1.2B disbursed |

**The March 2024 Package — Why It Worked:**
The simultaneous announcement of:
1. EGP devaluation to market rate
2. Massive interest rate increase
3. $35B UAE investment confirmation
4. IMF program resumption

...created a "credibility package" that finally convinced markets Egypt was serious. The parallel market premium evaporated within days.

</details>

## 🌍 Geopolitical Timeline Affecting Egypt's Economy

<details>
<summary><strong>External Shocks Chronology</strong></summary>

| Date | Event | Economic Channel |
|------|-------|-----------------|
| Feb 2020 | COVID-19 global spread | Tourism −70%, remittances dip |
| Mar 2021 | Ever Given Suez blockage | 6 days; $9.6B/day trade disruption |
| Feb 2022 | Russia-Ukraine war | Wheat +60%, capital outflows |
| Oct 2023 | Gaza war begins | Tourism uncertainty, regional risk premium |
| Nov 2023 | Houthi Red Sea attacks | Suez Canal revenues at risk |
| Jan 2024 | Red Sea crisis intensifies | Major shipping lines reroute to Cape of Good Hope |
| Feb 2024 | UAE Ras El-Hekma | $35B stabilization anchor |
| Mar 2024 | EGP liberalization | Confidence restored, IMF program resumes |

**Red Sea Crisis Impact:**
- Suez Canal daily transits fell ~50% at peak crisis
- Some shipping lines added 10–14 days rerouting via Cape of Good Hope
- Egypt lost an estimated **$6-8B in annualized canal revenue** during peak disruption
- Military and diplomatic efforts ongoing to stabilize southern Red Sea

</details>
